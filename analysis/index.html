Satellite record versus modelled flood

These are two kinds of evidence and the site keeps them apart. Seen from space: floodwater detected by satellite between 1 July and 31 August 2022 (UNOSAT, VIIRS, pixels of about 375 m, not checked on the ground). Computed: river floods of a chosen likelihood, once in 10, 100 or 500 years, from the Copernicus GloFAS global model. The model describes a flood of a given odds, not the one that happened. The panels show the same 16 km window around , the tehsil with the most flooded mapped buildings (the most exposed tehsil has almost none mapped). Solid indigo is what satellites saw; amber hatching is what the model computes.

How well does each model flood match the satellite picture?

Area: the share of the observed flood area that falls inside the scenario area, and the share of the scenario area that was flooded in 2022 (Pakistan only). Buildings: the share of the OSM residential-like buildings inside the observed extent whose centre pixel, or any of the 3 × 3 pixels around it (about ±90 m, the analogue of the footprint buffer in the Dutch comparison), has a modelled depth of 0.1 m or more.

Source: outputs/modelled_vs_observed.csv (script 03_modelled_hazard.py)
The scenario is a river-flood model; the 2022 event was a monsoon flood with rain-fed and river flooding on a very flat plain. A scenario that matches the observed area well is not evidence that it predicts a given dwelling. Neither layer has an observed depth: the observed product has no depth, and the scenario depth is for the scenario only.

    Confidence in each input

    Population: modelled, not a census count

    WorldPop 2020 (100 m) is a statistical model of where people live, constrained to totals but not a count of people. Against the 2017 census totals of the tehsils in the study, the WorldPop sum is times as large (the years differ) and the two correlate at across tehsils on a log scale.

    Buildings: OpenStreetMap is incomplete

    The OSM export has buildings for the whole of Pakistan. In the study tehsils there are a median of mapped residential-like buildings per 1,000 WorldPop people (national: ), and study tehsils have none. Where a 100 m cell has no mapped building, method C counts the whole cell if its centre is in the extent, and of the people exposed under method B live in such cells. The building layer is therefore a lower bound and says nothing about housing type or storeys here.

    Flood extent: satellite water detection

    VIIRS has about 375 m pixels and the product is cumulative over two months and not field validated, so it describes where water was seen at some point, not the maximum depth or duration. Water under cloud or dense cover can be missed.

    Census: tehsil level, 2017

    The smallest area with public age data is the tehsil. The 2023 census has block-level data internally, but no download or boundary file was found. Age on the map is therefore a tehsil-level proxy from 2017.

    Can a regression explain exposure?

    The same tests as the Dutch run, on tehsils with complete predictors (65+, 0–14, 15–24, female share, household size, log density, mean elevation). Response: the area-weighted share of the tehsil's people inside the observed extent.

    Source: logs/run.log (07_tehsil_dataset_and_diagnostics.py)

    Distribution of the response across the complete cases

    Share of the tehsil's people inside the flood extent, in bins of 5 percentage points. The bar at 0 is cut at the top.
    Source: outputs/regression_sample.csv
    What the numbers say: unlike the Dutch sample, a regression is technically possible here. There are tehsils with complete data, almost none with zero exposure, and a local model fits the response closely. That fit should be read carefully. The response is itself derived from a raster and a flood polygon, it is strongly clustered in space (Moran's I above), and the strongest single association is with elevation (Spearman in the table). A local regression would largely describe where the flood was, not why people were exposed. The vulnerability predictors available at tehsil level are few (age, household size, density), and none of health, income or housing quality was obtained.
